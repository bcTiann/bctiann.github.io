PERSONAL PROJECT · OCTOBER 2026

GeoRA Reimplementation

I reimplemented geometry-aware low-rank adaptation for reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards, then compared GeoRA and LoRA under the same short training budget.

The method comes from Zhang et al., GeoRA: Geometry-Aware Low-Rank Adaptation for RLVR (ACL 2026). My work covers adapter implementation, GRPO training, controlled evaluation, and generation–training integration.

My implementation →Original paper →Results & protocol (JSON) →

What I implemented

  • GeoRA initialization and forward computation, with frozen residual weights and trainable low-rank adapters, integrated into the TRL GRPO training loop.
  • A matched LoRA comparison, an untrained GeoRA initialization control, independent answer scoring, and checkpoint save/reload checks.
  • A synchronous vLLM/TRL bridge that exports the effective policy weights and verifies the policy loaded by the generation worker before each update.

Training and evaluation

Base modelQwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct
Training taskGSM8K: 7,345 training questions, with a separate 128-question development split
Equal adapter budgetAll-linear targets excluding the output head; rank 16, alpha 32; 18,464,768 trainable parameters for each method. GeoRA rho: 0.2.
Training budget25 updates and 3,200 sampled responses per method (16 questions × 8 responses per update); seed 42; AdamW with learning rate 10−6
PrecisionBF16 base/frozen residual weights; FP32 trainable adapters and optimizer states
EvaluationAll 500 MATH500 questions; one greedy response per question; at most 4,096 new tokens; Math-Verify 0.8.0 mathematical-equivalence scoring

The checkpoint at update 25 was fixed before MATH500 evaluation. The 128-question GSM8K development results are separate from the MATH500 results below.

GeoRA scored 3.0 percentage points above LoRA: 43 additional correct answers and 28 lost answers, a net gain of 15/500.

Paired bootstrap 95% interval: −0.2 to +6.2 points; exact two-sided McNemar p = 0.0959.

This is a short, single-seed experiment, and the paired interval includes zero. It gives a positive signal for follow-up rather than an established advantage. GeoRA also starts from a different numerical initialization: the trained model is only 1.0 point above its untrained control. That control was reconstructed and checked against the saved frozen residuals, rather than loaded from an original update-zero checkpoint.

vLLM/TRL integration check

I separately tested synchronous generation and learning on two devices, initializing each adapter from update 25 with a fresh optimizer. Each method completed 2 updates and 256 responses, with a 512-token response limit.

The checks covered effective-weight export, loaded policy versions 0 → 1 → 2, finite sampling probabilities and gradients, changed adapter tensors, and exact checkpoint reload. All 512 responses across both methods were audited. This validates the integration; the shorter response limit and dense diagnostics make it unsuitable for a speedup comparison with the original training run.
