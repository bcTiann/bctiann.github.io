PERSONAL PROJECT · OCTOBER 2026

SuperMimic Reproduction

I trained a learned dynamics model and humanoid motion-tracking policy on 40 LAFAN1/G1 clips, then evaluated every scheduled checkpoint across the full motion set.

This project builds on SuperMimic and Motphys’ Uni-SuperMimic implementation. My work focuses on running and checking the model-training loop, preserving the effective training budget, and evaluating motion coverage.

Policy rollout

What I worked on

The world model learns from simulator transitions. The actor is trained through 32-step imagined rollouts, using gradients through the learned dynamics rather than through the physics simulator.

  • Ran the joint world-model, actor, and critic training loop with the aggregate data and effective optimization budgets derived from the public upstream defaults.
  • Implemented actor microbatching while retaining all 32 imagined steps and accumulating gradients before one effective optimizer update; checked losses, gradients, optimizer state, and sample ordering against the full-batch path.
  • Added explicit training/sample counters, finite-state and checkpoint checks, and complete 40-motion evaluation at all 13 scheduled checkpoints.

Experiment protocol

Motion set40 LAFAN1 clips retargeted to the Unitree G1; the same clips are used for training and evaluation
Training run12,000 learner iterations, 4,096 environments, and 122.88 million new training transitions; an additional 262,144 transitions for initial warmup
Effective model budget120,000 world-model updates; 11,500 actor updates through 32-step imagined rollouts; 230,000 critic updates
Training time24.77 hours; checkpoint evaluations ran separately after training
EvaluationAll 40 clips, once from their first frame without added noise; mean normalized return across clips, with full-length completion reported separately
RuntimeCPU MuJoCo physics and FP32 PyTorch MPS training; the upstream reference uses CUDA/mjwarp and BF16

The 11,000-iteration checkpoint completed all 40 motions with a mean normalized return of 0.825997, including all six fall-and-get-up clips.

The final checkpoint had a higher mean return but one jump ended at 98.2% of its reference duration. These are two distinct checkpoint results.

The 11,000-iteration checkpoint was selected after evaluating the same training clips. This single-seed run measures tracking coverage on those 40 motions; it does not establish unseen-motion generalization or real-robot performance. The Motphys row is its published reference, not one of my runs. Matching the public aggregate budget does not make the backends, numerical precision, or sampling trajectories identical.

What I checked

I reconstructed each evaluation’s clip-wise mean, frame-weighted score, completion count, and failure duration from the saved results. The final run reached its planned transition and optimizer-update counts, and all 13 full-motion evaluations completed. A separate CPU reload confirmed that all 191 saved tensors were finite, and source/data identity checks passed.

The comparison uses the fixed Motphys public revision. The small 40-clip experiment is separate from the much larger motion library reported in the original SuperMimic paper.
